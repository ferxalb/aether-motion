# AETHER Motion architecture

Source layout and resource limits of the native renderer. Compositions with `data-hf-split-wrap` are parsed twice (`parse::parse_file_with`): the renderer measures the first parse's unwrapped rows and the second parse builds the wrapped ones, so line breaks come from the same fonts and layout that draw them. Contributor rules (CPU budget, benchmarking, tests) live in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md); measurements live in [BENCHMARK.md](../BENCHMARK.md).

## Source layout

- `src/model.rs`: parsed composition, styles, and time-sampled motion tracks.
- `src/parse/`: HTML/CSS parser; unsupported features fail with explicit errors. `mod.rs` is the `parse_file` facade; `css/` (stylesheet, selectors, cascade, variables), `values/` (lengths, colors, backgrounds, clip paths and masks, filters, transforms, shadows), `style/` (layout, text, and visual property groups), `animation/` (CSS animation, `data-hf-keyframes`, and `data-hf-camera` into motion tracks, including animated filter amounts; `rig.rs` reads the `data-hf-camera-*` rig options, turns rig keyframes into `camera::RigKey`s, and resolves each subject after the walk; `shots.rs` compiles `data-hf-shots` presets into rig keys), `rich_text.rs` (inline `<span>`/`<strong>`/`<b>`/`<em>`/`<i>`/`<code>` runs folded into their element's text as `model::TextSpan`s), `split/` (`data-hf-split` kinetic text: `mod.rs` builds one flex row of pieces, or a column of line rows for text with `<br>`, wrapping, line motion, or line masks, with optional per-word clip boxes; letters record the next letter in `LayoutHints::kern_after`; `motion.rs` holds the start values, stagger orders, and eased entrance tracks of pieces and lines; `SplitPlan` carries the measured line breaks of `data-hf-split-wrap` from the first parse to the second), `effects.rs` (`data-hf-bloom` and per-element `data-hf-motion-blur` into `model::ElementEffects`, the keyframed bloom intensity, and the checks that reject per-element blur with the root shutter, nested, or on the root), `captions.rs` (`data-hf-captions` rows of per-word highlight pieces built from a transcript), `transition.rs` (`data-hf-transition` scene transitions, compiled into pose, filter, and pixel-effect tracks), `variables/` (`data-composition-variables` declarations and value coercion in `declare.rs`, and the `data-var-text`, `data-var-src`, and `data-var-if` bindings in `bind.rs`, applied to the DOM before the stylesheet is read; the `--id` custom properties are pinned on the composition root through `Stylesheet::pinned`), `tree.rs` (DOM walk), `timeline.rs` (`data-start`), `audio.rs` (`<audio>` attributes into `AudioClip`s, kept outside the element tree), `audio_effects.rs` (`data-hf-volume-ramp`, `data-hf-eq`, and `data-hf-duck` for `<audio>` and video sound, and the trigger id resolution), `html.rs` (node helpers).
- `src/camera/`: the cinematic camera rig, independent of HTML and pixels. `mod.rs` holds `CameraRig` (zoom in log space, focus, framing anchor, and follow weight as curves, evaluated from time; `Motion::pose` uses it in place of the camera's translate and scale tracks), `curve.rs` the per-channel curve (monotone cubic Hermite `smooth` segments with automatic tangents, or keyframe easings), and `tracking.rs` the subject path, whose dead zone and lag are baked once at load on a fixed 240 Hz grid and read back with a Catmull–Rom spline. `render/layout/camera_subjects.rs` binds each rig to its subject after the final parse (resting layout from the renderer's own placement), so tracking depends only on the composition. `render/layout/box_transform.rs` runs just before it: it turns each static `translate(%)` into a constant `Motion::offset`, folds `@keyframes` percent tracks (`Motion::percent`) into the `x` and `y` tracks, and resolves `transform-origin` into `Motion::origin` (the origin's px offset from the box center), all from the element's resting border box.
- `src/render/`: software renderer. `mod.rs` holds the public `Renderer` and frame loop; `draw.rs` walks the scene; `layout/` sizes and places flex items; `paint/` holds the `Surface` pixel buffer and its primitives (fills, gradients and soft shadows backed by a memory-bounded tile cache in `tile_cache.rs`, borders, outer and inset box shadows, backdrop blur, element effects in `effects/` (the offscreen layer for `filter` blur, color functions in `color.rs` (also the root grade), `drop-shadow`, `mix-blend-mode` compositing in `blend.rs` (separable and non-separable modes), the tint, channel split, and glitch pixel effects of `model::FxMotion` in `fx.rs`, the `data-hf-bloom` halo in `bloom.rs` (bright pass box-averaged into a 1x, 2x, or 4x reduced grid, blurred there, bilinearly upsampled, tinted, and added with an ordered dither after the element is composited; its planes live in a `BloomScratch` reused across frames), and the continuous blur of animated filters in `exact_blur.rs`; effect layers other than a static blur share the frame's tile caches, frozen while both drawing passes run), images with cached row spans, `clip-path` and `mask-image` layers in `masked.rs` (shape coverage from the exact-area polygon rasterizer in `coverage.rs`, mask alpha from `gradient.rs`), rotation, the vignette/grain `finish` pass, `float_ops.rs`, exact call-free `round`/`hypot` replacements for per-pixel loops, and `kernels.rs`, branch-free per-row kernels (two-stop gradient rows, radial gradient parameters, cached gradient texels, shadow masks) compiled for the baseline target and for AVX2 and chosen at run time, bit-identical by construction and tested against the scalar loops they replaced; `finish.rs` dispatches its row loops the same way); `text/` handles fonts (`fonts.rs`: requests by family list, weight, and italic flag, keeping face bytes for outlines and marking italic requests that resolved to an upright face), pair kerning for `font-kerning: normal` (`kerning.rs`: GPOS pair adjustment lookups of the `kern` feature or the legacy `kern` table, memoized per pair, plus `Renderer::text_kerning`, the per-character adjustments shared by layout and painting), the synthetic oblique (`oblique.rs`: a 14 degree shear of glyph coverage around the baseline; strokes shear their outlines), inherited properties and per-run styles of inline rich text (`props.rs`: `RunStyle`, `style_index`), glyph strokes from font outlines (`stroke.rs`, `ttf-parser` outlines stroked by `tiny-skia`), line wrapping (`wrap.rs`), block layout (`block.rs`), glyph painting with fixed-size coverage masks and subpixel resampling (`mask.rs`) for continuous translation/zoom, and `text-shadow` (`shadow.rs`, one mask per layer, painted back to front); `shutter.rs` averages sub-frame samples for `data-motion-blur`; `layer_blur.rs` averages one subtree for `data-hf-motion-blur` (it plans the sample times each frame, then draws each sample over the static background already painted, restores only the touched pixels between samples, and averages them in an integer accumulator that grows to what the samples touched); `layout/split_wrap.rs` places a `data-hf-split-wrap` element at rest and scale 1 (`Renderer::rest_rect`, sharing `draw::place` and `draw::content_box` with drawing) and measures its words with the flex sizing used for drawing, between the two parses; `occlusion.rs` skips the siblings drawn before a scene instance that covers the whole frame with an opaque, directly drawn background (only on the frame itself, never in layers, samples, or probes); `text/shared.rs` gives elements whose bodies and inherited chain are the same shared bodies one text cache key, so reused scenes rasterize their text once; `allocation.rs` checks frame/layer byte sizes and coordinate conversions; `geometry.rs` and `frame_state.rs` hold small shared types.
- `src/space/`: 2.5D spaces, independent of HTML and pixels. `mod.rs` holds `Stage` (projection and the camera's dolly and orientation curves), `PlaneMotion` (a plane's static and keyframed depth and tilts, and `data-hf-backface`), and `Depth` (an element's role, kept in `ElementBody::depth`); `math.rs` holds `Affine` (CSS-ordered plane transforms composed down the tree), `View` (perspective or orthographic projection from a camera that dollies and turns, near clipping, back-face tests), the plane homography and its inverse, and polygon clipping in plane coordinates; `bsp.rs` orders planes with a BSP tree built in paint order over each plane's paint extent, splitting planes that cross into fragments that carry their cuts as plane-local half-planes (bounded at 4096 pieces and 256 levels, past which `render/space.rs` sorts whole planes by distance). `parse/space.rs` reads `data-hf-space`, `data-hf-perspective`, the plane attributes, keyframed `z`/`rotateX`/`rotateY`, and the camera's `z`/`rotateX`/`rotateY`/`rotate` from `data-hf-camera` keyframes or `data-hf-shots` (`shots.rs` compiles their keys), and checks that planes sit in a space. `render/space.rs` builds a `SpaceIndex` once per renderer: the stages (each `data-hf-space`, and each element inside a space that draws its subtree on a layer and holds planes, which flattens them into a nested space), and for each stage its nodes in paint order with resting offsets, sizes, parallax factors, and the extent and content of each plane's flat descendants; every frame `draw_space` composes the transforms from the element states, orders the planes, and draws whole camera-facing planes through the normal pipeline at a projected placement (`Renderer::draw_plane` overrides `posed_rect`) and everything else through `render/paint/project.rs`, which draws one `PlaneLayer` per plane over its visible part and warps each fragment through the inverse homography (trilinear mipmaps, cuts decided at pixel centers so split pieces partition the pixels) and reports its text to the layout probe through a `ProbeMap`. Planes with `data-hf-motion-blur` go through `layer_blur.rs`'s `blur_samples`, which places them again at every sample. A space camera's keyframes leave its box at rest (`posed_rect`); `render/layout/camera_subjects.rs` bakes a followed subject's 3D path and depth magnification (`camera::Gain`). `FrameState::depth` and `FrameState::camera_3d` carry the 2.5D values so the identical-frame shortcut and the root shutter see depth motion.
- `src/scene3d/`: native low-poly 3D (`data-hf-3d`), independent of HTML and pixels. `mod.rs` holds `Scene3d` (nodes in document order, each a group, mesh, light, or camera with a `Transform` of `Channel` curves: translation, CSS-ordered rotation, and per-axis scale, composed with `space::math::Affine`), materials, lights, and the camera settings; `mesh.rs` the indexed `Mesh` and the procedural primitives (plane, box, pyramid, cone, cylinder, icosphere), all outward-facing; `shade.rs` flat Lambert and unlit shading in linear light; `camera.rs` the `View` at one instant (eye, orthonormal axes from the node or a level aim at a `target`, yaw/pitch/roll) and the `ClipMatrix` shared by every backend (reversed depth: 1 near, 0 far); `frame.rs` evaluates a scene at one instant into the shared representation, world-space triangles that face the camera with their flat colors, plus the on-screen travel used for motion-blur sample counts; `raster.rs` the deterministic CPU rasterizer (clip-space clipping against near, far, and a guard band; 1/256-sample fixed-point edges with the top-left rule and an exact per-row span solver; per-sample `f32` depth; 64 px tiles of `antialias^2` samples resolved to straight-alpha rows for a sink); `gpu.rs` (feature `gpu`) the wgpu backend drawing the same triangles with the same matrix and an integer resolve shader, read back into the same sink; `backend.rs` the backend request (`--3d-backend`, `AETHER_3D_BACKEND`), the sticky CPU fallback and its reason, and the per-renderer `Workspace` (triangle list, raster tile, GPU target, and a one-entry memo that replays an identical draw, such as the second pass of an effect layer). `parse/scene3d.rs` reads the host's `data-hf-3d` options and its `<hf-node>`, `<hf-mesh>`, `<hf-light>`, and `<hf-camera>` descendants (attribute lists checked per tag, keyframe channels into `Channel`s), and `parse/tree.rs` stores the result as `Content::Scene3d` without walking those children. `render/paint/scene3d.rs` places the viewport (padding box at output resolution, camera zoom and `--scale` included), composites the rows with the element's opacity, and reports scene travel to the shutters; `FrameState::scene_3d` carries `Scene3d::key` so the identical-frame shortcut and both motion blurs see 3D motion. See [3D](3d.md).
- `src/captions/`: transcript domain, independent of HTML and rendering. `transcript.rs` imports `transcript.json`, OpenAI Whisper and whisper.cpp JSON, SRT, and VTT into timed words; `cues.rs` groups words into cues; `export.rs` writes SRT, VTT, and the word array. It never transcribes audio.
- `src/video/`: `<video>` sources. `colour.rs` reads SPS colour tags and converts non-default pictures; `index.rs` reads the MP4 sample tables (no decoding) into display-order frame times and closed GOPs at parse time; `player.rs` decodes on demand with OpenH264, seeking to the GOP keyframe and decoding forward (closed GOPs make decoder output order equal display order); `annexb.rs` converts length-prefixed NAL units. `parse/video.rs` builds `Content::Video` (and, unless `muted`, an `AudioClip` for the source's AAC track, decoded through the same path as `<audio>`), `render/paint/video.rs` fits and blits the frame, and each renderer fork owns one player per element.
- `src/vector/`: vector path geometry. `measure.rs` flattens SVG paths (parsed through `usvg`) into a polyline with cumulative lengths and samples points and tangents by distance; `draw_on.rs` holds `DrawOnSvg`, an inline `<svg>` prepared at parse time with a placeholder in each shape's `style`, which writes the dash pattern of every stroke for one instant. `parse/vector.rs` reads `data-hf-motion-path` (into `model::MotionPath`, added to the pose in `Motion::pose`) and `data-hf-draw` (into `Content::DrawnSvg`, rasterized per frame by `render/paint/images.rs` while drawing and cached once finished).

## Native rigid-body physics

`parse/scene3d/physics.rs` reads the `<hf-body>` authoring contract. After the parser resolves
the viewport duration, `scene3d/physics.rs` uses a compile-local Rapier world to produce bounded,
immutable pose tracks at 120 Hz. The shared `Scene3d` owns the bake, so imported scene instances
and renderer workers read the same tracks. World transform evaluation supplies the interpolated
poses to meshes, lights, and camera targets. Frame identity includes the poses, and native 3D
shutter travel checks intermediate ticks within the exposure to account for reversals at impact.

## External scene compilation

`parse/scenes/` loads each document independently using the same parser and a bounded canonical-path stack (`cache.rs`). The stack caches each compiled scene by canonical path, resolved variable values, and parse pass (a scene without `data-hf-split-wrap` text serves both passes); a later instance clones it after repeating the depth, instance-count, and cycle checks against the recorded subtree, and renumbers or verifies its `data-hf-split-wrap` ordinals so the result equals a fresh compile. `model::Element` keeps per-instance data (scoped id, tree links, clip times) next to a shared copy-on-write `ElementBody` (`Arc`, written through `DerefMut`), so cloning a compiled scene copies no styles, tracks, or content; only a fitted root and subject-following cameras get their own body. A `SceneCache` made with `SceneCache::persistent` (used by `preview --watch` through `parse_file_cached`) keeps the scenes of a successful compile and drops them before the next one when a fingerprint (`fingerprint.rs`) of any file they read changed; `parse/reads.rs` records the paths a compile tries to read, so a failed one can be watched. A document's flattened element count is capped (200,000) as instances are added. `data-composition-fit` places a scene of another canvas size with a static uniform scale on its root. Each parse pass also records `Composition::dependencies` (documents and the assets their elements, audio, and captions read), which `preview --watch` polls. Each instance becomes a full-canvas clip containing the imported element tree. The compiler offsets element times and remaps parent, child, camera-subject, and audio-ducking indices while preserving clip-local motion tracks. Scoped IDs describe the flattened tree. A shared wrap plan keeps both parse passes aligned across imports, and camera tracking binds once against the final layout. Scene cutoffs use the existing ancestor visibility rules and an audio timeline cap. See [Motion Scenes](motion-scenes.md) for the authoring contract.

## Memory and resource limits

Composition, external scene, and variables files are limited to 16 MiB before they are read, and the MCP server accepts JSON-RPC messages of at most 32 MiB per line. The system font index is scanned once per process (`render/text/fonts.rs`) and cloned by each renderer.

The renderer keeps one RGBA frame, up to 64 MiB of scaled images, and up to 32 MiB of text masks. When the visible state matches the previous frame, it reuses the RGBA frame and the native encoder's YUV conversion.

Each gradient or outer-shadow tile cache retains at most 65,536 keys, including first sightings and rejected tiles. Exceeding that metadata limit returns an explicit render error; existing slots are not evicted or changed, preserving the cached/direct drawing decisions. Reduce animated size variation or split the composition when this limit is reached. Fitted-image and text-run caches also retain at most 4,096 entries; image eviction and uncached text preparation retain the same pixel calculations. Text-layout blocks already have a 4,096-key cap.

Frames and offscreen RGBA layers keep their 128 MiB per-buffer limit, with checked multiplication and widened coordinate subtraction before allocation. Rotation and element blur reject unrepresentable pixel coordinates. Shadow-edge profiles are limited to 16 MiB before allocation. These are per-resource bounds, not a guarantee of total process memory or successful allocation under memory pressure.

### Fitted SVGs under cache pressure

The fitted-image cache uses the same 64 MiB and 4,096-entry limits for raw and packed pixels
combined. On byte pressure, it attempts to pack resident SVGs and subsequent fitted SVGs of
at least 512 KiB. `paint/packed_image.rs` stores the original straight RGBA in independent
lossless LZ4 row bands. It keeps packed data only when it saves at least one quarter of the
original storage. The blitter reuses a 256 KiB buffer and unpacks only bands with rows it needs
to paint. Geometry, fitting, alpha math and cache keys remain the same, so cold and warm renders
use the same pixels. Bitmap and sequence entries retain raw fitted pixels. Cache eviction can
still occur when the combined working set exceeds the limit.

### Vector artwork under animated zoom

While an element's scale (or an ancestor's) is changing, `FrameState::zooming` is set from a
1 ms look-ahead of the pose, a pure function of time. Vector images with `object-fit: fill` are
then drawn from a zoom-ladder level (`paint/svg_levels.rs`): an exact raster at
`2^(k/8)` times the unscaled box, never smaller than the frame's fit, resampled down by at most
one ladder step (≈9%, plus up to one pixel of per-axis rounding) with a separable fixed-point
Catmull-Rom filter on premultiplied samples. The ladder applies only while that level raster fits
the 64 MiB per-image limit; a fit within the limit whose level would exceed it (up to about 19%
more pixels) is rasterized exactly at every new size, as before the ladder.
Levels are ordinary exact entries of the fitted-image cache (keyed by `Fitting::Exact`, packed
under pressure as above), and resampled fits are keyed by `Fitting::Level`, so the pixels never
depend on what is cached. Resampled fits are evicted first and never push out an exact raster;
when they would, they are drawn once without being stored. Frames at rest, `contain`, `cover`,
raster images, and sequences are fitted exactly as before. A vector fit larger than the 64 MiB
per-image limit is rasterized only for its part on the current surface (`svg::rasterize_region`)
and not cached; that part must itself fit in 64 MiB.

`AETHER_PROFILE=1` reports SVG parse and raster phases, bitmap fit, image packing, blit
(time includes blending and unpacking), frame finishing, and fitted-image cache counters.
Peak fitted bytes are the largest residency of any worker, and raster-byte counters count
logical fitted allocations. Use the operating system's time tool for process peak RSS.
`AETHER_IMAGE_PACK=off` selects the raw cache for profiling and output comparisons.

## Cinematic layers and finishing

`parse/sequence.rs` validates numbered still-image files and builds an immutable path table. `FrameState` includes the cel index so a new cel invalidates the identical-frame shortcut. `paint/images.rs` decodes on demand into a per-renderer 64 MiB FIFO cache shared by all sequence elements. The existing 64 MiB fitted image cache holds fitted cels and static images or SVG plates. Both caches cap their entry count at 4,096. Cel resizing filters premultiplied components and converts back to straight alpha for the existing image blitter. Static image fitting retains its legacy behavior. The temporary resize plane is limited to 128 MiB. Cached pixels and the frame chosen at a given timestamp are independent of cache history.

The camera's pan contribution (`Motion::camera_pan`, from the tracks or the rig) travels in `FrameState` when an immediate child uses parallax. `draw.rs` corrects each such child's origin and flex slot before its own pose, clipping or effects. The existing camera zoom, static transform, and motion path still apply. Parallax needs no additional surface. A changed scene still composites its visible layers. Fitted artwork stays reusable when its dimensions stay fixed and the cache budget can retain it. Animated zoom or working sets larger than the fitted cache can cause rerasterization. A rig changes nothing in drawing: it only supplies the camera's pose, so a dolly costs what a classic animated zoom costs (each zooming frame fits SVG plates at a new size; the cache stays within its budget).

Element filters and compositing run first. The existing localized bloom derives a halo from premultiplied visible pixels and adds it to the destination; a mask on the bloom element also confines its halo. If enabled, the root shutter averages the rendered samples. The root CSS grade is applied to the resulting frame, followed by the optional per-channel filmic tone curve (`parse/filmic.rs`, `paint/filmic.rs`). Filmic controls use the sRGB transfer function, apply exposure as a linear gain, a shadow toe and a highlight shoulder, and return to sRGB through a 256-entry LUT. The curve operates on an already composited 8-bit frame. It cannot recover highlight detail clipped earlier. Vignette and grain are applied last.

## Video

A `<video>` element is indexed when the composition is parsed: the container's `stts`, `ctts`, and `stss` tables give each frame's presentation time and the keyframe groups, so the clip length and the frame shown at any time are pure functions of the composition. The frame shown `local` seconds into the clip is the last source frame whose time is not after `media_start + media(local)`, where `media` is the clip's speed curve (the identity at 1x) (looped sources wrap by the source length; otherwise the last frame holds). The source frame index is part of the element's `FrameState`, so the identical-frame shortcut still applies when the source runs slower than the output.

Decoding happens at render time, per renderer: a request for frame `k` starts a fresh decoder at the keyframe of its GOP (or continues when `k` is ahead in the same GOP) and decodes forward, so sequential rendering decodes each frame once and a backward jump costs up to one GOP. Rotation metadata is applied when the picture is converted. Sources with open GOPs, B-frames (any frame shown before a frame stored ahead of it; the bundled OpenH264 2.7 decoder returns an out-of-memory state on every such stream), fragmented MP4s, mirroring, and non-H.264 codecs are rejected at parse time. The decoded picture is converted to RGBA at source size (`video/colour.rs` reads the SPS matrix and range; limited-range BT.601 uses the decoder's own conversion, everything else, including the limited-range BT.709 that the native encoder writes, a fixed-point integer one), fitted to the element box with `object-fit`, and blitted opaque (no tile cache). Memory per player is the current picture; each render thread has its own players, so 4K sources cost roughly 33 MiB per picture per thread. A 1080p source costs about 23 ms per new source frame on one thread (decoding, conversion, and blit; see `BENCHMARK.md`).

## Audio

Audio is built once, on the calling thread, before the video is encoded, so the result does not depend on `--threads` (AAC encoding costs about 2.5% of real time per audio track on one core, thanks to the FFT-based MDCT in the vendored `oxideav-aac`; see `BENCHMARK.md`). The mix has two `f32` planes (8 bytes per sample frame, capped at 512 MiB, about 22 minutes); each decoded source is capped at 128 million samples (512 MiB) and is held once at 48 kHz per distinct file.

Timing is exact by construction. Times become samples with `round(seconds * 48000)`, the track spans `round(frames * 48000 / fps)` samples, and AAC frames of 1024 samples are written after the video frame during which they start. The encoder primes its first frame with 1024 zero samples; an `elst` edit list (media time 1024, duration equal to the video) hides them, and `mvhd`/`tkhd` durations equal the video's. The `mp4` crate writes no edit list, so `audio/mux.rs` rewrites the final `moov` box after the file is closed. Tests decode the MP4 audio and check a burst lands on the expected sample (see `tests/audio.rs`), and macOS `afconvert` decodes the same file to the same sample.

## Encoding

Both encoders produce H.264/yuv420p at a constant frame rate. The native encoder (OpenH264, through the `openh264` wrapper vendored in `vendor/openh264` with a `base_qp` option, see its `AETHER-PATCH.md`) runs without rate control at the fixed quantizer of the `--quality` preset (`encode::Quality`: draft 30, standard 26 (the default), high 22, max 18); `standard` keeps the quantizer of exports made before presets existed. Frames are converted by `encode/yuv.rs` to limited-range BT.709 4:2:0 (fixed-point integer math, 2x2-averaged chroma, identical bytes on every CPU and thread count), and the SPS VUI signals BT.709 primaries, transfer, and matrix with `video_full_range_flag` 0. `--encoder ffmpeg` uses FFmpeg from `PATH` with `veryfast` and the matching CRF (28, 25, 21, 18) (video only: compositions with `<audio>` need the native encoder). Audio is AAC-LC, 48 kHz stereo, at 192 kbit/s by default. For native exports at 1080p or higher, `--threads 2` or more overlaps drawing and color conversion with encoding through a one-frame queue.

Distributed rendering builds on the segment pipeline. `render <file> --output part.aseg --segments A..B` renders and encodes segments `A..B` (one second each; `inspect <file> --fps N` prints the count) into a part file: a small header (output size, fps, quantizer, frame and segment counts, range), the SPS/PPS, and the MP4 samples. `render <file> --output video.mp4 --parts a.aseg,b.aseg,...` checks that the parts cover every segment exactly once with the same settings and parameter sets, mixes the audio once from the composition (pass the same `--variables`), and muxes the samples with the same `Muxer` as a single render, in about a millisecond per part. Renders of one segment or less cannot be split. Separate processes or machines can therefore render ranges of one export in parallel; the API (`aether-motion-api`) shards long renders across workers this way. `render <file> --output 'frame-{frame}.png' --frames 0,45,90` writes several stills from one process.

The MP4 of assembled parts equals a single render, byte for byte, for any worker count and split. Each worker's tile caches (`render/paint/tile_cache.rs`) still decide from their own history whether a whole-pixel gradient or soft shadow is drawn from a cached tile, but an uncached draw computes exactly the texels the tile holds (gradient color x256 and alpha x4096 in box-relative coordinates; shadow coverage x65535 relative to the element), only for its visible pixels, and blends them with the same function, so the decision never changes a pixel (`tests/distributed.rs` checks 1, 2, and 4 threads and a two-part split on drifting glows and a moving shadowed card). The effect-layer `freeze`/`thaw` of the caches is kept, although with identical paths it now only affects which draws are fast.
