# Native physics and volumetric lighting research

The original baseline at `a6a5887` shipped fixed and dynamic box and sphere bodies, baked at
120 Hz. A follow-up added launch diagnostics, pose-keyed kinematics, revolute joints, and a CPU
light shaft with one explicit box occluder. The [current authoring contract](3d.md) describes
those features. The proposals below cover extensions such as global fog, additional casters,
timed impulses, and joint motors.

## Recommendation

Keep Rapier in Rust and extend the existing pose bake. Build on position-based kinematic
bodies with authored impulses, offset compound colliders, and joint motors. For rendering,
use the hinged lamp's depth and shadow fixtures to extend the bounded shaft toward homogeneous
fog and single scattering with general geometry casters.

Volumetric lighting computes how light travels through fog, dust, or smoke at one instant. A rigid-body solver computes how objects move over time. They meet at the evaluated world transforms. A simulated body can obscure a beam, carry a spotlight, or occlude fog just like a keyframed body.

## Volumetric lighting

### 1. Establish a CPU reference with homogeneous fog

Use one neutral extinction coefficient `sigma_t` in inverse scene pixels and a bounded range. Expose a half-transmission distance for authoring, where `sigma_t = ln(2) / distance`. Start with one static medium per viewport, an opaque sky color, and a fog color that specifies a spatially constant source function. This models distance fog under uniform illumination and has a closed form with no marching loop. Reserve scattering albedo and anisotropy for the pass that uses scene lights, and report the resolved medium extent and coefficient through `check`.

For a segment of world length `d`, transmittance is `T = exp(-sigma_t * d)`. With a constant source function `S` (radiance toward the camera per unit extinction), outgoing radiance is `T * L_end + (1 - T) * S`. The endpoint is the first opaque sample, the end of the medium, or the far plane, as appropriate. On a ray with no opaque hit, use the authored sky as the boundary radiance after integrating the medium. This also gives a direct analytic reference for later marching. [PBRT, transmittance](https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Volume_Scattering/Transmittance) · [PBRT, equation of transfer](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Transport_II_Volume_Rendering/The_Equation_of_Transfer)

- Reconstruct camera-axis depth from the same terms as `ClipMatrix`. For reversed depth `z = a/d + b` in perspective, invert with `d = a/(z-b)`. For orthographic `z = a + b*d`, use `d = (z-a)/b`. Convert perspective axis distance to distance along the normalized ray. Use the actual subpixel sample location and viewport placement, including cropped regions and output scaling. Start integration at the near clip plane and treat the clear depth sentinel as a miss.
- Keep transmittance and in-scattered light in linear radiance through the 3D sample resolve. Preserve the original raster path for scenes without fog. Within the volume path, zero extinction must give unit transmittance and zero in-scattering. The filmic curve currently sees an 8-bit composited frame, so map the linear result to the existing output format at the 3D boundary and test highlights there. The initial implementation can fit within the viewport without a composition-wide HDR change. [Existing filmic order](architecture.md#cinematic-layers-and-finishing)
- For the first release, require a solid native sky for fog viewports so the whole volume and its background can resolve together in linear light. The viewport can still fade, mask, and transition as a 2D layer. The 2D layers behind it have no native 3D depth. Later, a transparent volume needs a deliberate linear compositing path carrying additive radiance and transmittance through layer operations. For example, 50% neutral absorption on white should encode to around 188 in sRGB8. Multiplying the encoded value by 0.5 gives 128.
- Use reproducible math for exponentials, phase functions, and sRGB conversion in the new reference path, including any table generation. Test against numerical oracles and hash CPU output across the supported architectures before claiming cross-platform byte equality.

Give the medium integral its own module under `scene3d` and put ray reconstruction next to `View` in `camera.rs`. Let `shade.rs` supply unclamped linear surface radiance for medium viewports while preserving the existing encoded triangle path. At the raster tile boundary, supply the medium resolve with sample depth and target coordinates, including clear samples. Average the resulting linear samples and encode once per output pixel. An opaque native sky supplies the miss color and makes a partially covered silhouette a mix of fogged surface and fogged sky. Test zero extinction against that linear resolve baseline, and test byte identity separately on legacy viewports.

This first slice should show depth separation and clean silhouettes. A beam-shaped volume or a fog overlay cannot certify geometry-shadowed light shafts.

### 2. Add local media, a directional light, and actual occlusion

Start with a bounded homogeneous box and one directional light. Compute a light-space depth map from transformed shadow casters **before camera-facing culling**, with a frustum covering the receivers and relevant off-screen casters. Reuse the CPU coverage and depth primitives where their semantics match. Use a bounded shadow buffer and a fitted, texel-snapped light projection over the shot or authored volume bounds. Expose bias and resolution through diagnostics first. Check for acne, detached shadows, unsupported coverage, and shimmer during camera movement. The shadow map supplies solid visibility for fog and can also feed opaque-surface shadows as the surface shading path evolves.

For the camera ray, intersect the volume, then truncate it at the opaque surface. March a deterministic set of intervals near to far. At each midpoint estimate the incident light, check the light-space shadow map, apply the scattering coefficient and phase function, and accumulate front-to-back. For piecewise constant extinction in a step of length `h`, use `T_step = exp(-sigma_t * h)`, add `T_so_far * (1 - T_step) * S_step`, and multiply `T_so_far` by `T_step`. Where feasible, use the analytic light-path length inside the homogeneous box to attenuate incident light, and test opaque blockers separately. Add Henyey–Greenstein angular scattering with an explicit convention for the cosine between incoming and outgoing light travel directions. Handle the near-zero extinction limit and constrain anisotropy away from singular endpoints. [PBRT, volume scattering](https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Volume_Scattering) · [Frostbite, physically based volumetric lighting](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/index.html)

Next, add a spotlight with a smooth cone edge and a perspective shadow map. This supports practical lights such as a lighthouse, stage fixture, or searchlight. Point lights need omnidirectional shadows and are a later step. For convincing lighting on large surfaces, evaluate local-light attenuation, cone falloff, and shadowing at the visible sample position. A color evaluated once per triangle makes the result depend on tessellation. Keep flat normals if the shot calls for the engine's low-poly look.

For a small correctness oracle, trace rays from selected volume samples to the light against the same pre-cull triangles. Compare visibility away from shadow edges to the shadow map. This isolates light leaks caused by missing casters, clip coverage, or bias from errors in the volume integral. Define a caster's sidedness independently of its camera-facing material cull. In the first spotlight shot, attach the light to a motorized rigid body and put a second moving body in the beam. Verify shadows from both visible and off-screen geometry and sample the beam during shutter exposure. A moving light or off-screen caster can change the picture even when the projected bounds of visible meshes barely move, so the shutter needs an explicit lighting-change signal or a fixed sample floor for that shot.

### 3. Scale volume quality without depending on previous frames

| Approach | Use in AETHER Motion |
| --- | --- |
| Analytic fog | First implementation and an oracle. Cost is proportional to visible samples and there is no step count to tune. |
| Bounded ray march | CPU reference for single-scattering shafts. Profile volume bounds, empty-space skipping, early transmittance termination, lookup tables, and deterministic depth-aware upsampling before increasing defaults. |
| Frustum-aligned volume grid (froxels) | Later GPU option. Inject extinction and lighting into a coarse 3D grid and integrate down each column. Reconstruct at surface depth with depth-aware spatial filtering. Keep the frame independent of prior frames. The temporal reprojection in interactive implementations assumes history that snapshots and split exports do not have. [Unity HDRP, SIGGRAPH 2018](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2018/Siggraph%202018%20HDRP%20talk_with%20notes.pdf) |
| Sparse volume assets and offline reference rendering | Useful once shots need heterogeneous smoke, clouds, or fire. Evaluate importing pre-baked density, later emission, with signed-off bounds and dependency tracking. NanoVDB is a candidate read-only asset format. Use PBRT or a dense CPU march to compare visual quality in small reference scenes. [NanoVDB FAQ](https://www.openvdb.org/documentation/doxygen/NanoVDB_FAQ.html) · [PBRT, volume integrators](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Transport_II_Volume_Rendering/Volume_Scattering_Integrators) |

At 1080p and 2×2 supersampling, 24 steps across the full viewport are about 199 million step evaluations per shutter sample. At 4K that is about 796 million, before shadow lookups. These are workload counts. Historical [3D benchmarks](../BENCHMARK.md#020-native-low-poly-3d-2026-11-21) put the existing Lighthouse opaque pass at 37.0 ms/frame at 1080p and 150.7 ms/frame at 4K at 2×2, single-threaded, with bloom removed. Benchmark incremental cost with a matched scene, reporting coverage, steps, shadow resolution, shutter samples, working memory per renderer, and the load/bake time separately. At one volume ray per 4×4 block and 32 steps, the 1080p work is about 4.15 million step evaluations before reconstruction. Compare thin occluders, silhouettes, and camera-motion stills with the reference before adopting a coarse grid.

Apply CPU-only availability before workers paint any frames. Report the selected backend and reason consistently in check, preview, and export. Reject a GPU-required export if any reachable volume needs CPU, even if the first rendered frame shows no volume. Keep one backend for the entire export. Add wgpu support first by retaining depth through a separate volume and resolve pass, then CUDA if cloud measurements justify it. Measure volume-path interior and edge error separately, since the existing GPU tolerance covers primarily triangle edges and depth ties.

Budget GPU intermediate buffers before choosing the resolve design. At 4K a full-screen buffer with four f32 depths per output pixel takes 126.6 MiB, before sample colors, shadow maps, or readback. Reuse a sampleable depth attachment where supported and compare tiled processing or a coarse volume grid if attachment or storage limits are reached. The CPU resolve can keep its current one-tile depth footprint.

## Native rigid-body physics

### Extend the immutable pose bake

**Rapier 0.36.1 is already pinned** with `enhanced-determinism`. Its position-based kinematic bodies infer contact velocity from successive poses, and its collider and joint APIs cover the next authoring milestones. Keep one solver and a fixed tick through those milestones. **Jolt** is a C++ candidate if Rapier misses a measured contact, joint, or bake-time requirement. Compare both on the same fixtures and compare pose hashes within each solver across target architectures. An optional C++ backend would add a native build, runtime distribution checks, and a narrow C ABI at the compile-time bake boundary. The viewport renderer would still read the immutable tracks. [Rapier rigid bodies](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/rigid_bodies/) · [Rapier determinism](https://www.rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/determinism/) · [Jolt architecture](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysicsDocs/5.7.0/) · [Jolt build options](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/md__build_2_r_e_a_d_m_e.html)

| Milestone | Proposed scope | Acceptance scene and measurements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Directed contact | The engine now submits the next kinematic target before each Rapier step. Add timed impulses for dynamics with integer tick diagnostics and stable document order for ties. Use explicit units, and keep dynamic pose keys invalid. | A keyframed pusher transfers momentum to a resting prop. An on-cue impulse releases a second prop. Check free-fall, before/after impact, timed events, and matching hashes after seeks and scene reuse. |
| 2. Collider fidelity | Support a bounded set of offset, rotated primitive colliders within a body. Define one mass rule and validate the combined center of mass and inertia. Evaluate convex hulls against this baseline and reserve triangle-mesh colliders for static scenery if added. | A prop with an off-center mass tips around the expected contact. A rolling prop and a thin-wall CCD fixture expose geometry mismatch and tunneling. Measure stack penetration, rest jitter, and bake cost. [Rapier colliders](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/colliders/) |
| 3. Articulated motion | The engine now resolves revolute joints with local anchors and angular limits before baking. Add bounded motor targets and apply motor updates before each step. Try Rapier's reduced-coordinate joints for long articulated chains only if the initial constraint-based joints miss the measured drift budget. | A motorized lamp and a loaded hinge stay within travel and anchor tolerances. Test reversals and long holds. Use angular error, anchor separation, and residual oscillation to compare solver settings. [Rapier joint constraints](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/joint_constraints/) |

For an impulse at clip time `t`, a provisional rule is to assign it to the first tick boundary at or after `t` and apply it before the step starting at that boundary. A cue at tick zero changes velocity before the first step. A cue at the last visible boundary has no subsequent visible displacement. For kinematics, sample the target at the end of the same step. Keep order stable when multiple commands share a tick and reject references to missing, disabled, or non-dynamic impulse targets. Start with linear or smooth kinematic interpolation and a shared deterministic pose constructor. Arbitrarily easing a collider through another body can inject unrealistic velocities, so verify per-tick displacement on the authored path before broadening easing support.

The [parser](../src/parse/mod.rs) already bakes after the local clip duration is resolved, and [scene evaluation](../src/scene3d/mod.rs) replays interpolated translations and quaternions. A shortened imported instance trims playback of the shared bake. The frame key includes simulated poses, and shutter travel examines the intervening ticks. Keep these paths shared as body types grow. An impact can still reverse motion within a single tick, so compare blur at the contact against a higher-rate reference before promising accurate streaks for fast, thin props.

The current payload is `28 * (fixed_count + dynamic_count * (ceil(duration * 120) + 1))` bytes. Fixed bodies store a single sample. At 60 seconds, 83 dynamic tracks fit in the 16 MiB viewport cap and 84 need about 16.15 MiB. Before adding more bake types, account for total retained pose bytes across the compilation, counting shared scene instances once and separately compiled variable bindings individually. Include kinematic tracks and budget collider and joint counts independently of body count. Report solver version, effective speed limits, resolved event ticks, and aggregate bytes alongside the existing `scenes3d[].physics` output. Benchmark compile and seek separately. [Current bake](../src/scene3d/physics.rs) · [Current diagnostics](../src/agent.rs)

Any higher sample rate or solver change can alter contacts. Compare finer sampling on the same fixtures, with unchanged output hashes on existing scenes as a regression check. If a persistent bake cache becomes necessary, key it by normalized inputs, variables, asset fingerprints, solver version, timestep, and bake format. Compare deterministic compression to raw tracks before considering checkpoint replay, which needs the solver's contact and warm-start state.

### Findings from the integrated engine

- **The parser accepts speed values beyond the solver's effective limits.** In a one-second viewport containing a lone dynamic sphere, `velocity="80000 0 0"` and `velocity="1000000 0 0"` both advanced 333.333 px on the first tick, equivalent to 40,000 px/s. A launch at 20,000 px/s advanced 166.667 px. At 3,600°/s about z the first tick turned 29.957°. At 21,600, 43,200, and 100,000°/s it turned 44.856° in all three cases. Rapier 0.36.1 defaults to a 400 m/s linear limit and an angular limit derived from 45° per full step, with linearized rotation integration. AETHER sets the timestep and keeps those defaults. `inspect` and `check` now show both limits and the velocities reported after tick one. Changing the defaults can alter CCD and existing trajectories. Even if fast rotation is enabled later, shortest-arc interpolation cannot reconstruct a turn greater than 180° between adjacent samples. [Rapier integration parameters](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/integration_parameters/) · [AETHER body parsing](../src/parse/scene3d/physics.rs)
- **A contact stack repeated bit for bit locally.** An integrated test probe parsed 64 boxes of size `50 50 50` px arranged in a 4×4×4 array above a fixed floor. Centers followed `(60x−90, −38−58y, 60z−90)` for indices 0–3, with friction 0.6, restitution 0.15, and CCD. The floor was `600 20 600` px centered at `0 20 0`. The 10-second scene stored 2,152,220 bytes of poses. The first bottom box's center was −14.973 px at 9 s, compared with the ideal resting center of −15 px. Across 12 compiles in two processes the FNV-1a hash `481d544c0d9d4d08` repeated over all world-transform translations and row-major matrices at ticks 0–1200, encoded as little-endian f64. Debug-profile parse plus bake took 683–884 ms on Intel macOS with Rust 1.100.0. These measurements cover one platform and one load case.

### Local Rapier feasibility probe

A standalone release-mode Rust probe used Rapier 0.36.1 with `enhanced-determinism`, default solver settings except for a 1/120 s timestep and +9.81 m/s² y gravity, and stable body insertion order. It dropped 64 half-meter cubes in a 4×4×4 grid onto a static box with top at y = −0.1 m. Cube centers were spaced 0.6 m on x and z and started at y = −0.38 m with 0.58 m between layers. Cubes used 0.6 friction, 0.15 restitution, zero initial velocity and CCD. The probe advanced 1,200 ticks and saved each body's position and quaternion, including tick zero. The first bottom cube finished within 0.03 m of its expected resting center.

On macOS x86-64 (Intel i5-10400F, Rust 1.100.0), two processes each built the world and baked it six times. The 76,864 poses matched bit for bit within each process and both processes returned the same FNV-1a checksum over the little-endian f32 component bits (`4127a06b35bd5c1b`). The raw pose payload was 2,152,192 bytes. Ten warm bakes took 9.7–11.1 ms each, including world setup and pose storage. The first bake in each process took 11.3–19.9 ms. This measures a small collision scene on one machine. Cross-platform pose equality, kinematic motion, AETHER Motion integration and volumetric performance all need their own measurements.

## Verification and first milestones

The original research checkout passed all 16 `scene3d` integration tests and the
`scene3d_gpu` comparison on an active wgpu adapter. The follow-up's `scene3d_physics`
suite covers launch diagnostics, hinge stability, box shadows beyond the camera silhouette,
and a byte-for-byte lamp export comparison across a split during the swing.

- **Fog reference:** prove depth inversion for both projections, including off-axis rays, cropped viewports, and camera-inside-volume cases. Check known transmittance at several distances, near/far clipping, zero extinction, partial coverage, no-geometry frames, and predictable sky and highlight color. Require unchanged legacy CPU fixture hashes. Use check and stills on a shot with distinct foreground, middle and far objects.
- **Rigid-body bake:** verify free-fall within solver tolerance, contact and restitution, stack rest, high-speed CCD, and a keyframed pusher. Compare pose arrays at exact ticks and intermediate samples, including reversed seek order, 30/60 fps at shared times, repeat scene instances, standalone snapshots, worker-count changes, and render splits. Compare bake and CPU still hashes on macOS, Linux, and Windows, including ARM64 and x86-64. Existing CI runs those operating systems, so add explicit architecture coverage where needed. Inspect impact stills and shutter samples.
- **Volumetric shadows:** use a moving occluder with a known ray and a blocker outside the camera frustum. Confirm that the volume draws on empty pixels, never contributes behind a nearer opaque surface, and follows the animated light and camera without history or edge shimmer. A moving lighthouse beam crossing haze and passing behind a foreground structure is a useful delivery-size shot once the spotlight is implemented.
- **Release checks:** follow the repo's still-based review workflow. Compare CPU and GPU output and cost at 1080p and 4K after feature parity, with different shutter counts. Update the 3D contract, architecture notes, diagnostics, limits, and the native 3D skill when each capability ships.
