# Limits

- SVG (`<img src="x.svg">` or inline `<svg>`) is static: no `<text>`, SMIL animation, scripts, `<foreignObject>`, external resources, or gzip-compressed SVGZ. Scripts of any kind remain unsupported by design (determinism); use the native `data-hf-*` motion instead.
- Descendants of a rotated element are clipped to its box plus its shadow reach. This is a deliberate, documented limitation.
- There is no CSS 3D transform (`perspective()`, `rotateX`, `matrix3d`, a depth in `transform-origin`); these return an explicit error. `skew()` and non-uniform `scale()` are static only: they cannot be animated, are rejected on the root, cameras, inline text runs, and `data-hf-space` stages and planes, and cannot sit alongside a CSS animation of `transform` (put them on a parent). `transform-origin` is rejected on the root, cameras, and stages. Percent translation (`translate(-50%, -50%)`) is resolved once from the element's resting border box (an animated size does not move it); it is rejected on the root, cameras, and stages. Depth comes from `data-hf-space` (2.5D planes) and real geometry from `data-hf-3d`.
- Native 3D (`data-hf-3d`) is low-poly and flat-shaded: meshes are opaque with one color each, lit by ambient and directional lights only. A CPU-only light shaft supports one explicit box shadow caster. Rigid bodies support centered box and sphere colliders and revolute joints. The renderer has no textures, PBR, smooth shading, per-material transparency, point or spot surface lights, general surface shadows, global fog, model files (glTF, OBJ), skeletal or morph animation, deformable physics, joint motors, 3D text, or second cameras. A scene holds at most 4096 nodes and 100,000 triangles. The optional GPU backend is close to the CPU but not byte-identical (see [3D](docs/3d.md)).
- `filter` functions may come in any order, with at most one `blur()` and one `drop-shadow()` (no `url()`). Blur and drop-shadow are not allowed on the root, and the root accepts no `opacity()`; children that stick out of the element by more than the blur and drop-shadow reach plus the element's shadow reach are cut. Filters animate on non-root elements only, and CSS `@keyframes` filter lists interpolate per function rather than switching discretely when their functions differ. An animated `drop-shadow()` uses one continuous kernel for its blur.
- `mix-blend-mode` supports every Compositing Level 1 mode (separable and non-separable, in sRGB) plus `plus-lighter`, never on the root, and never inside `overflow: hidden`, rotation, reveals, wipes, filters, or another blended element.
- Motion blur (`data-motion-blur`) is set for the whole composition on the root (no per-element opt-out), costs about one full draw per sample, and snapshots use 30 fps for the shutter window unless `aether_snapshot` gets an `fps`. Per-element motion blur (`data-hf-motion-blur`) averages one subtree; it cannot be combined with the root's shutter, nested, or put on the root, costs about one draw of its subtree per sample plus a copy of the frame per blurred frame, and has no per-pixel velocity (it samples whole instants, like the root shutter).
- `data-hf-bloom` is a threshold-and-blur halo added over the element: luminance threshold only (no soft knee or per-channel threshold), one radius per element (not animatable; only the intensity animates), multiplicative tint only, computed from the element's own pixels (other elements do not bloom unless they have their own `data-hf-bloom`), and drawn through the element's effect layer, so it shares that layer's limits (children outside the box plus the reach are cut). Wide halos are evaluated on a 2x or 4x reduced grid.
- `clip-path` takes basic shapes, `path()` (at most 20,000 flattened points), and box keywords, but no `url()` and no `stroke-box`/`fill-box`/`view-box` (an HTML element has none). It animates only through `data-hf-keyframes` between basic shapes of the same function, point count, units, fill rule, and box (paths, box-only clips, and `closest-side`/`farthest-side` radii do not animate), and it cannot be combined with `mix-blend-mode` on the same element. `mask-image` takes one linear or radial gradient over the border box (alpha mode only; no image masks, sizing, or repetition).
- `backdrop-filter` supports only `blur()`, and inside `overflow: hidden` or rotated elements it blurs that element's layer, not the whole scene.
- Scene transitions cover crossfade, push, cover, wipe, whip, zoom, dips to black or white, flash, blur, glitch, and RGB split. GPU shader transitions are unsupported; the light, focus, and glitch kinds are CPU effects with fixed shapes (no custom shaders, colors, or strengths per transition). Blurring transitions fade scene edges toward what is behind, like `filter: blur`. Wipes and `data-hf-reveal` clip to the element's box, so shadows outside it are cut while the reveal is partial, and they cannot be combined with rotate.
- `data-hf-draw` animates strokes only (no fill animation), works on inline `<svg>` only, re-rasterizes the SVG every frame while drawing, and replaces any authored `stroke-dasharray` while a shape draws. `data-hf-motion-path` uses a fixed curve subdivision (48 segments per curve) and holds its ends outside the travel.
- While a camera or scale animation zooms `object-fit: fill` vector artwork, each frame is resampled from a shared zoom-ladder raster (at most ~9% above the drawn size, plus a pixel of rounding) instead of rasterized at its exact size, while that raster stays within the 64 MiB per-image limit (otherwise the fit is rasterized at every new size); frames at rest are exact. A vector fit larger than 64 MiB is rasterized for its visible part each frame and not cached; a visible part above 64 MiB still needs `--scale`. Shapes clipped at that part's edge can differ from a whole raster by one antialiasing step (1/16 coverage) on a few pixels.
- Inline rich text supports `<span>`, `<strong>`, `<b>`, `<em>`, `<i>`, and `<code>` runs that change color, family, weight, style (italic), letter spacing, fill, stroke, or a `background-clip: text` background; runs cannot change `font-size`, `line-height`, or `font-kerning`, cannot be boxes (padding, borders, backgrounds), have no underline or links, and cannot animate on their own. Kerning applies within a run, not across a change of run. A run's gradient spans its extent on each line separately. Text strokes are not included in `text-shadow`.
- `data-hf-split` handles plain text with `<br>` breaks (no child elements; up to 400 pieces). `data-hf-split-wrap` breaks only between words (no hyphenation or breaking inside a word), needs a definite width at rest, and costs a second parse of the composition plus a font load to measure. Masks clip to the line box, so descenders need `line-height` of about 1.15 or more. Letters kern only with `font-kerning: normal`.
- Kerning (`font-kerning: normal`) applies pair adjustments only (GPOS pair positioning or the legacy `kern` table): no contextual or cross-run kerning, no ligatures or other shaping, and no kerning next to whitespace. The default `auto` does not kern. A synthetic oblique (italic text without an installed italic face) is a fixed 14 degree slant.
- `box-shadow` takes up to 8 layers, outer or `inset`; `text-shadow` takes up to 8 non-inset layers without spread, each costing one blurred mask of the text run.
- `<video>` sources must not use B-frames (frames reordered for display); the built-in OpenH264 decoder cannot decode them, so they are rejected with a re-encode command. See [benchmarks](BENCHMARK.md#video-clips) for decode measurements.
- Audio: WAV, FLAC, Ogg Vorbis, and MP3 sources, mono or stereo only; AAC-LC output at 48 kHz stereo (no surround, no HE-AAC). Fades are linear or equal power, a loop crossfade (`data-hf-loop-crossfade`) is for `<audio>` at 1x only (a `<video>`'s sound loops hard with its picture), volume automation (`data-hf-volume-ramp`) interpolates linear gain, ducking reacts to sample peaks or windowed RMS (no LUFS loudness detector or compressor), the master limiter is a fixed 5 ms look-ahead, 100 ms release peak limiter on sample values (no true-peak oversampling), the equalizer has no dynamic or linear-phase bands and its filter state starts at zero at each clip start, and `--encoder ffmpeg` cannot carry audio. Dither is opt-in TPDF without noise shaping. The mix is capped at about 22 minutes. Variable speed (`data-hf-speed`, `data-hf-speed-ramp`) is varispeed: pitch follows the rate (no pitch-preserving time stretch), and video slow motion repeats source frames (no frame interpolation).
- Composition variables are fixed for a whole render: they cannot change over time, cannot be read by scripts (there are none), and bind only through `var(--id)`, `data-var-text`, `data-var-src`, and `data-var-if`. Set `data-variable-values` on each external scene instance to override its source defaults. `step`, `placeholder`, `label`, `description`, and `brandRole` are editor metadata and are not enforced. `font` values are family names resolved like `font-family` (no font `source` URLs), and `image` values are local paths (no `fit`). A number's `unit` reaches CSS only when it is `px`, `%`, `deg`, `turn`, `rad`, `grad`, `s`, or `ms`; there is no `calc()`.
- External scenes start at local time zero and can be cut shorter. A different canvas size requires `data-composition-fit`. For nesting limits and outer-composition finishing, see [Motion Scenes](docs/motion-scenes.md).
